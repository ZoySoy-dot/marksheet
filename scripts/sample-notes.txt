CHEM 102 - Lecture 7: Chemical Kinetics

Reaction rate is the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.
For aA + bB -> cC, rate = -(1/a)d[A]/dt = (1/c)d[C]/dt.

RATE LAWS
The rate law is rate = k[A]^m[B]^n. The exponents m and n are the reaction orders
with respect to each reactant. They are determined experimentally and are NOT the
stoichiometric coefficients. The overall order is m + n.

The rate constant k depends on temperature but not on concentration.

INTEGRATED RATE LAWS
Zero order:   [A]t = -kt + [A]0      plot [A] vs t is linear
First order:  ln[A]t = -kt + ln[A]0  plot ln[A] vs t is linear
Second order: 1/[A]t = kt + 1/[A]0   plot 1/[A] vs t is linear

HALF LIFE
For a first order reaction the half life is t(1/2) = 0.693/k, and it is independent
of the starting concentration. This is why first order half lives are constant.
For second order, t(1/2) = 1/(k[A]0), which does depend on starting concentration.

COLLISION THEORY AND ACTIVATION ENERGY
Molecules must collide with sufficient energy and correct orientation to react.
The minimum energy required is the activation energy, Ea.
The Arrhenius equation is k = A*exp(-Ea/RT), where A is the frequency factor.
Raising temperature increases k because a larger fraction of molecules exceed Ea.

CATALYSTS
A catalyst increases reaction rate by providing an alternative pathway with a lower
activation energy. It is not consumed in the reaction and does not change the
position of equilibrium or the enthalpy change of the reaction.
